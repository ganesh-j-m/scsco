Dirty Politics in Maharashtra |

As soon as we got freedom from the bondage of the British rule, it was expected that
there would be an emergence of the golden era in the Indian politics. But as the days, years
passed, it turned out to be a misconception for the common man. Here [ am talking about the
politics in Maharashtra which has seen corrupt and violating judicious precedents set by the
erstwhile Indian social reformers, policy makers and freedom fighters. I, being a common
citizen of this democratic nation, I feel sad when I think about the present political
developments taking place in the cultured Maharashtra. Political drama that took place in
the two consecutive two years i.e. in 2022 and 2023 is beyond the understanding of the
common man.

As soon as the results for the 2019 assembly election declared, Three political
parties came together to form the govt. in Maharashtra but the fact was that they wanted to
keep the B.J.P. away from the chief ministererial post. But this coalition did not last long.
Because one of Shivsena party’s confidant left the Shivsena party with around forty MLAs
which resulted in the collapse of Maharashtra Vikas Aghadi (A collaboration of three
political parties).

My purpose is not to blame any political person or party but to bring it to the notice of
the people that politicians are only craving for status and power. Politics in Maharashtra in
this respect is only a microcosm of the Indian politics. The same picture more or less is seen
across the country. As we are celebrating the 75 anniversary of Indian Independence; it is
noticed even after 75 years of Independence that almost all political parties are focusing
only on how to grab the chair of the power for the power. We the Indian people still believe
in democratic values like equality fraternity and justice. The common men should come
together to elect the best political persons to maintain the ever cherished Indian culture.
Then there will be the real celebration of the 75 th years of our independence.

Women Empowerment after Independence

The status of women in India has undergone a great change since the nation’s
independence in 1947. The independence marked the beginning of women empowerment
in true sense. It was only after the independence that the women’s position in India was
taken under consideration and their problems were started being addressed. Before 1947,
India was not in a position to look into internal matters such as that of women’s status and
their rights as it was engaged in the freedom struggle. In Post-independence, the nation
embarked on a journey of empowering women and ensuring their equal participation in all
spheres of life.

Over the decades there has been a significant progress in women’s education. After
independence the government implemented several policies to promote women education.
The establishment of educational institutions, scholarships, various campaigns to promote
women education has led to a significant increase in female literacy rates. Today the number
of women pursuing higher education is found to be greater than the number of men pursuing
higher education. These days, the number of girls attending college lectures regularly is also
higher than the number of boys attending college lectures. This shows that women
are aware of the importance of education.

Women have also been contributing to the economy of India by running various
businesses. Today there are many women entrepreneurs who have great initiatives about
setting up their own enterprise but what they lack is professional training. Thankfully the
government has implemented various schemes to promote women entrepreneurship and
skill development training. Programs such as the National Rural Employment Guarantee
Act (NREGA) have provided opportunities for rural women to engage in gainful
employment and improve their economic status.

The political sphere of India also has the participation of women. The Constitution
of India guarantees equal political rights to men and women. The landmark Panchayati Raj
Act of 1993 mandated the reservation of seats for women in local government bodies,
leading to increased participation of women in grassroots governance. Today, women
occupy important positions in politics, including the Presidency, Prime Minister’s office,
various ministers, etc. While the women empowerment has brought several positive
changes, gender disparity still exists in rural and urban areas of India. There are several
other challenges like domestic violence, sexual harassment which need to be resolved.

Based on the above information it can be concluded that women empowerment has
truly helped us in raising the status of women in India. It is important that we continue
empowering women to bring the best out of them and to let their potential be of great help to
our nation’s development.

Spread of English language in India after Independence |

English language came to India with the advent of East India Company in India which
was started by the Englishmen in 1600. Even though it’s been 75 years since we got
independence, the English language still holds a position of great importance in the Indian
society. The English language which was once considered only as the language of the
colonisers has now become an unwavering and irreplaceable part of the Indian society. It
has had an interesting journey in India since the country gained its independence in 1947.
Together with Hindi, English is used as the official language of the Indian government.

Initially, English was introduced by the Britishers as the language of administration
and governance. It gradually became the language of the elite and the educated class. After
the independence, the Indian government faced a challenging task, which was to unite a
linguistically diverse nation. With hundreds of languages spoken across the country, it was
necessary to find acommon language for communication and administration.

One of the key factors contributing to the spread of English in post-independence India
was the establishment of education system. English-medium schools, initially set up by the
British continued to flourish and gained prominence as symbols of quality education.
Furthermore, English language skills were also necessary for the growth of Indian economy
as India participated in the global market. English became the language of commerce and a
medium for communication with international partners. The media also played a significant
role in the spread of English in post-independence India. English newspapers, magazines,
radio, television became influential sources of news and entertainment. Indian authors
writing in English such as, R.K. Narayan, Mulk Raj Anand, Salman Rushdie, Arundhati
Roy, Amitav Ghosh, etc. also played a significant role in promoting English language in
India.

However, the spread of English language in India also created a linguistic division.
People proficient in English language have advantages over those who are not. People who
don’t know English are often frowned upon at places where English is spoken. Some also
argue that the English language and the adoption of the English culture takes us away from
our Indian culture. There have been debates about the promotion of regional languages. In
response to these concerns, the Indian government is taking efforts to encourage the usage
of regional languages while keeping in mind the importance of English as a global
language. In this way, the English language has had a long journey in India and today it plays
a crucial role in the field of education, administration, business and communication. The
knowledge of English language has definitely benefited us greatly.

Azadi ka Amrut Mahotsav
“Freedom is never given, itis won”

Azadi ka Amrut Mahotsav is a campaign started in joy of completing 75 years of
Indian Independence. It is an event organised by the Govt. of India. It includes a series of
events all over the country at different places. This event was started on 12 March 2021 at
Sabarmati Ashram and will be continued till 15 August 2023. It was organised by PM of
India Mr Narendra Modi. India is celebrating the glorious history of its culture and
achievement. The aim of this event is to create a vision for India 2047. The main motive of
these events is that every citizen of India should know about freedom struggle. Azadi ka
Amrit Mahotsav marked the beginning of the revival of India’s journey towards self
reliance and self respect. The aim this event is to realise the sacrifice given by our millions
of freedom fighters. At the same time we cannot forget those brave hearts from our defence
forces. “Har Ghar Tiranga, Har din Tiranga” is a campaign that aims to instil a sense of pride
and love for our nation. It keeps reminding the citizens that they live ina colourful nation
breeding love, unity and diversity. This is a great approach to instil a sense of love,respect,
pride and responsibility towards our nation among the Indian citizens, kids and adult. It is an
important step to revive the culture and history of the Indian Independence.

Today we all know that freedom is something that money can’t buy because it is
achieved through struggle of many braver hearts. 15 th August is a day of pride and joy for
every citizen of our country. Today it is our responsibility to maintain the legacy by doing
simple deeds such as keeping cleanliness, planting trees, and by helping each other.
Mahatma Gandhi began ‘Dandi Yatra’ on 12 th March 1930 form Sabarmati Ashram.
(Purpose is to keep awaking self reliance and self respect in his nation.)

facing some of the biggest problems as Poverty, Global warming, Terrirism. It is a very

people used to think that girls can not complete boys. But today Indian girls proved
themselves that they have rights like men. They have shown their abilities not only in India
but also in the whole world, girls like PV Sindhu, Indira Gandhi, Kalpna Chawla etc.

Today India is known as the biggest democratic nation and fastest growing economy
and is a land of various cultures and a rich heritage. In India, people of different castes and
religion not only live together but also celebrate their festivals together. It means they share
their thoughts, love, and knowledge with each other. By keeping in mind those sacrifices
given by our freedom fighters for India. there is our biggest responsibility to maintain its
heritage. Azadi ka Amrit Mahotsav through this programme, Indian citizens can remind the
sacrifice given by those people to get Independence. No one can forget those uncountable
freedom fighters who fought and died for country’s freedom and the one and only Indian

Indian Education System After Independence |

We can see very conspicuous change in education system during these 75 years.
Number of colleges and schools have increased rapidly. In 1947 14 universities were
working, in the present there are 1,026 in numbers and primary schools increased from
140,794 to 774,742, India’s post-independence education system has grown leaps and
bounds to develop as one of the world’s largest education systems with 250 million children
in K-12 education and 40 million youth in higher education. Yet there are challenges to
deliver quality education to all its450 million children and youth.

August 15, 2022 is being celebrated ‘Azadi Ka Amrit Mahotsav’ to mark 75 years of
free India, here’s a look at Indian education in 1947 and in 2022. Planning Commission was
constituted in 1950 by the government of India to prepare a blueprint for the development of
different aspects of life, including education. Thereafter, successive five-year plans were
drawn up. The action was taken to achieve universal elementary education, to eradicate
illiteracy, start vocational and skill training programs, upgrade standards and modernize all
stages of education, with special emphasis on technical education, science, and
environmental education and provide facilities for delivery of high-quality education in
every district of the country.

From the 1950s to the ’80s, the number of educational institutions increased In India.
Primary schools, especially, experienced rapid growth because the states gave highest
priority to the universalization of elementary education in order to fulfil the constitutional
directive of providing universal, free, and compulsory education for all children up to the
age of 14. Independent India faced challenges in education system. Among them, a high
dropout rate with minimal continuation from primary to secondary education. A large
number of schools were understaffed and lacked adequate facilities.

A government resolution was passed in 1986 stating that all children who reached the
age of 19 years by 1990 would have five years of formal schooling or its equivalent. Plans
were also made to improve and expand adult and non- formal systems of education.

In 1947, on the higher education front, the nation had established 20 universities and
496 colleges. In 1948, the Indian Government established the University Education
Commission to supervise the growth and improvement of higher education.

Today, the country is looking at a massive overhaul of its education system with the
implementation of the National Education Policy 2020.

Student Charter :

By National Assessment & Accreditation Council (NAAC), Bangalore

Every stakeholder of higher education has a role to play for quality enhancement and sustenance. The ho
stakeholders such as the Government, management, teachers, students and the external quality assurance
agencies—have an important role in ensuring quality of higher education. The National Assessment and
Accreditation Council (NAAC) strives to play a catalytic role in synergising the stakeholders efforts.

Of all the stakeholders mentioned above, the students have a unique role to play in ensuring quality of higher
education institutions (HEIs). Firstly, the students should realize that they have the right for quality education and it
is the responsibility of the HEIs to provide quality educational experience to learners. Secondly, the students should
equally be aware of their responsibilities which in turn will help the institutions to provide quality education. This
means that the students should demand quality education and demonstrate their commitment to quality education
by accepting their responsibilities. Otherwise the HEIs will have very little motivation for quality enhancement.

An initiation has been made by the National Assessment and Accreditation Council (NAAC) in this direction by
developing a sample student charter for all higher education institutions. It is hoped that the charter will help
educational institutions to articulate their obligations to learners in clear terms and can be used as a benchmark for
institution's accountability to learners. The NAAC advocates the public display of student charter in every
institution as an act of promotion of student participation in Quality Assurance.
A) Institution's responsibilities towards students - The institution shall

o provide clear information to students about admission and completion requirements for all
programmes, the fee structure and refund policies, financial aid and student support services
eo ensuresufficientand well-run support services to all students
e promote healthy practices
B) Student's responsibilities of learning - The student shall
